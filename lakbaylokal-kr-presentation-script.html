The script

0:001. Opening Show the full page: the wavy banner and the form on the left.

Good day everyone. Our system is LakbayLokal. "Lakbay" means journey and "lokal" means local. It helps a traveler pick a Philippine destination and plan the trip day by day.

A system like this has to hold knowledge about places, travelers and money, and then reason with it. Today I will show four ways of representing that knowledge: logical, semantic network, frames and production rules. Each one is running inside this system right now.

1:002. Logical representation
FactsRules		 Keep Cebu, November, 3,500 PHP. Point at the "Best of Cebu" card: "✓ Fits your ₱3,500 daily budget".

Logical representation states knowledge as facts and rules that are either true or false.

Here are facts the system stores about Cebu: destination(Cebu), tag(Cebu, food), typical_cost(Cebu, 3000), avoid_month(Cebu, September).

And here are facts about this traveler: budget(traveler, 3500) and month(traveler, November).

RULE: fits_budget(T, D) :- budget(T) >= typical_cost(D) RULE: season_risk(T, D) :- month(T) in avoid_months(D)

3,500 is at least 3,000, so fits_budget is true, and the card shows a green check.

2:302. Logical (proof by changing a fact) Change the budget to 1500. Then put it back to 3500 and change the month to September.

Watch what happens when I change a fact. The budget is now 1,500, so fits_budget becomes false and the card shows a warning: the typical day is above your budget.

Back to 3,500, but now the month is September. Cebu has September in its avoid months, so season_risk is true and the system warns about typhoon or rainy season. Same rules, different facts, different conclusion. That is the strength of logic: it is precise and it can explain why.

3:303. Semantic network
NodesLinks		 Set the month back to November. Pick Tagaytay from the destination list. Scroll to "Trips You Might Also Like".

A semantic network stores knowledge as nodes connected by labeled links. It is good for relationships.

Tagaytay is a node. Look at the links leaving it: Tagaytay is near Taal Heritage Town, about 45 minutes. Near Anilao, about one hour. Near Manila, about an hour and a half. Near Laiya, about two hours. Near Verde Island, about three hours.

Each of those is a node too. Taal Heritage Town is a culture place. Anilao is a beach. Verde Island is an island. Each node also links to its highlights and to the kind of traveler it suits, such as Divers or Families.

The system follows the "near" links, keeps the ones within about three hours, and picks different types so the suggestions are diverse.

5:003. Semantic network (follow a link) Click "Show trips for Manila" on the Manila card.

I can walk the network. I click Manila, and now Manila becomes the starting node with its own links and its own built-in trip ideas. That is the advantage of a network: moving from one concept to the next is just following an edge.

5:304. Frames
SlotsDefaultsProcedures		 Pick Cebu again. Click "Build this trip", then open the "My plan" tab. Click "+ Add a stop" and show the form fields.

A frame groups everything known about one concept into slots. The plan you see is a Trip frame. It contains Day frames, and each Day contains Stop frames.

This form is the Stop frame. Its slots are time, title, area, category, duration, cost and note. Category has a fixed set of allowed values, and cost defaults to zero, which the system shows as "Free".

Frames can also carry procedures. The day cost is not typed by anyone. It is computed from the stops, which is an if-needed procedure.

7:004. Frames (procedures firing) Add a stop "Breakfast" at 06:30 costing 150. It jumps to the top. Then add a stop costing 3000 and watch the day meter turn red.

I add a breakfast at 6:30. The if-added procedure re-sorts the stops, so it moves to the top of the day.

Now I add something expensive. The Day frame recomputes its cost, compares it with the daily budget slot, and the meter turns red with the amount over budget. The trip card also updates the total and the budget left. Nobody wrote those numbers. The frames worked them out.

8:005. Production rules
IFTHEN		 Go back to "Recommended trips". Select the interest "Quiet/less-crowded places" and watch the order of the suggestions change.

Production rules are IF-THEN pairs. The system has a small rule base that fires whenever the traveler changes something. Here are the main rules:

IF a destination tag matches a chosen interest THEN +3 points IF budget >= typical cost THEN +3 ELSE IF budget is within 80% of cost THEN +1 ELSE THEN penalty IF month is a best month THEN +3 IF month is an avoid month THEN -4 IF "less crowded" is chosen THEN + (4 - crowd level) IF pace is Relaxed THEN 4 stops per day IF it is the last day THEN no lodging stop IF a day costs more than the daily budget THEN show the meter in red IF a destination has no built-in trips THEN find nearby places

I just chose "less crowded". That rule fired, quiet places gained points. Vigan moved to the top and Sagada joined the list, so the ranking changed.

9:006. Putting it together Show the comparison table below on screen, or switch to lakbaylokal-frames.html.

All four work together in one request. Rules score the destinations. Facts store what is true about each place. The network connects each place to nearby ones. Frames hold the trip and compute its totals.

9:307. Closing Return to the banner.

To sum up: logic gives precise facts and explainable answers, networks give relationships and travel links, frames give structure with computed values, and rules give flexible decisions. Together they let LakbayLokal recommend, plan and warn. Thank you. We are happy to take questions.

Likely questions and short answers

QuestionAnswer
Is this real AI?The recommendations come from stored knowledge and rules, a classic knowledge-based system. An optional Claude button exists, but everything shown today runs without it.
Where do the prices come from?They are planning estimates written into the system, not live prices. The page tells the user to confirm before booking.
How are travel times found?From the distance between two places, with road or boat speeds and a short list of known ferry links. They are estimates.
Why four representations?Each fits a different kind of knowledge: facts and tests, relationships, structured objects and decision rules.
What would you add next?More destinations with built-in trips, live prices and weather, and a rule to avoid rainy months automatically when the traveler says "Any time".
